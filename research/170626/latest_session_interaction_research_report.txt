LATEST SESSION-INTERACTION RESEARCH REPORT
AVAXUSDC — Japan/Asia, Europe, and US/NY Sessions
Research version: V4.1
Complete session days: 729
Dataset period: June 2024 to May 2026

============================================================
1. RESEARCH OBJECTIVE
============================================================

The objective of this research is to determine whether the completed Japan/Asia and Europe sessions provide a useful directional bias for the following US/NY session.

The research is not yet an entry/exit backtest. It measures whether specific pre-US session conditions are associated with the structural outcome of the US session.

The latest research design is used exclusively because it separates concepts that were previously mixed together:

1. Session structure
2. Session direction
3. Combined session context
4. Secondary quality ranks
5. Buffer acceptance and sweep information

The current US V3 inner/outer classification remains unchanged.

============================================================
2. LATEST VARIABLE DEFINITIONS
============================================================

A. STRUCTURE

Structure is measured only from session high and low relative to the completed reference session and its 0.5% buffer.

Japan/Asia reference:
- Immediately preceding completed US session.
- Exact consecutive-session continuity is required.

Europe reference:
- Current completed Japan/Asia session.

Structure categories:
- EXPANSION_UP
- EXPANSION_DOWN
- TWO_SIDED_EXPANSION
- CONTAINED_RANGE

An expansion requires price to exceed the relevant reference high or low by more than the 0.5% buffer.

CONTAINED_RANGE does not mean the session was small. It means neither side exceeded the reference session’s buffered boundary.

B. DIRECTION

Direction is measured from open to close, normalized by the session range:

session_efficiency = (close - open) / (high - low)

Direction categories:
- UP: efficiency > 0.10
- DOWN: efficiency < -0.10
- NO_DIRECTION: -0.10 <= efficiency <= 0.10

C. COMBINED SESSION CONTEXT

Each Japan/Asia and Europe session combines structure and direction.

Examples:
- EXPANSION_UP_UP
- EXPANSION_UP_DOWN
- EXPANSION_DOWN_DOWN
- CONTAINED_RANGE_UP
- CONTAINED_RANGE_DOWN
- CONTAINED_RANGE_NO_DIRECTION

This allows the research to distinguish structural reach from open-to-close directional control.

D. QUALITY RANKS

The quality ranks are calculated using prior eligible observations only.

Default reference:
- Previous 90 eligible observations
- Minimum prior history: 30 observations
- Fixed terciles:
  - Bottom third = LOW
  - Middle third = MEDIUM
  - Top third = HIGH

Quality variables:
- Range rank: how volatile the session was
- Direction-strength rank: how strongly price moved from open to close
- Expansion-strength rank: how far price exceeded the 0.5% buffer
- Reference-overlap rank: how much the current session retained the reference range

E. US OUTCOME

The US session retains the existing V3 inner/outer framework.

Primary outcomes:
- US_DIRECTIONAL_UP
- US_DIRECTIONAL_DOWN
- US_BOTH_SIDE_EXPANSION
- US_INTERNAL_LIQUIDITY_TAKE
- US_TRUE_CONTRACTION

============================================================
5. RESEARCH HYPOTHESES
============================================================

H1:
Europe open-to-close direction has a statistically significant relationship with the following US directional outcome.

H2:
Europe structure and direction provide a stronger signal when they agree than when they conflict.

H3:
Japan direction is weaker than Europe direction as an independent predictor, but Japan agreement strengthens the Europe signal.

H4:
Higher Europe direction strength increases the probability that the US session follows Europe’s direction.

H5:
Europe acceptance beyond the Japan 0.5% buffer strengthens directional continuation into the US session.

H6:
Range rank, expansion-strength rank, and overlap rank provide strong standalone directional signals.

H7:
When Europe structure and direction conflict, open-to-close direction should generally receive more weight than structural wick expansion.

============================================================
6. PRIMARY DESCRIPTIVE FINDINGS
============================================================

A. EUROPE DIRECTION IS THE MAIN US BIAS

Europe UP:
- Sample: 291
- US directional up: 57.0%
- US directional down: 18.2%

Europe DOWN:
- Sample: 356
- US directional down: 59.3%
- US directional up: 15.4%

Across all directional Europe sessions:
- US followed Europe: 58.3%
- US directly reversed Europe: 16.7%
- Remaining outcomes were non-directional US outcomes: 25.0%

Japan direction was materially weaker:
- US followed Japan direction: 44.2%
- US directly reversed Japan direction: 29.1%

Conclusion:
Europe is the primary pre-US directional input. Japan is better treated as confirmation or context.

B. MATCHING EUROPE STRUCTURE AND DIRECTION IMPROVES THE SIGNAL

Europe EXPANSION_UP_UP:
- Sample: 185
- US directional up: 64.3%
- US directional down: 13.0%

Europe EXPANSION_DOWN_DOWN:
- Sample: 208
- US directional down: 65.9%
- US directional up: 10.1%

Conclusion:
Direction provides the bias, while matching structural expansion confirms the bias.

C. CONTAINED SESSIONS CAN STILL HAVE DIRECTION

Europe CONTAINED_RANGE_UP:
- Sample: 67
- US directional up: 47.8%
- US directional down: 28.4%

Europe CONTAINED_RANGE_DOWN:
- Sample: 84
- US directional down: 47.6%
- US directional up: 25.0%

Conclusion:
Contained sessions are weaker than aligned directional expansions, but they are not automatically directionless. Keeping structure and direction separate was the correct design choice.

D. STRUCTURE-DIRECTION CONFLICT

Europe EXPANSION_UP_DOWN:
- Sample: 41
- US directional down: 48.8%
- US directional up: 26.8%

Europe EXPANSION_DOWN_UP:
- Sample: 30
- US directional up: 36.7%
- US directional down: 33.3%

Conclusion:
The bearish conflict case supports giving more weight to open-to-close direction than upside structural reach. However, the opposite conflict case is nearly balanced. The evidence is asymmetric, so the general rule is only partially confirmed.

E. JAPAN AGREEMENT STRENGTHENS EUROPE

Japan UP + Europe UP:
- Sample: 134
- US directional up: 65.7%

Japan DOWN + Europe DOWN:
- Sample: 156
- US directional down: 69.9%

When Japan and Europe conflicted:

Japan DOWN + Europe UP:
- Sample: 124
- US followed Europe up: 48.4%

Japan UP + Europe DOWN:
- Sample: 165
- US followed Europe down: 49.1%

Conclusion:
Japan agreement materially increases conviction. When Japan conflicts with Europe, the Europe bias remains present but becomes much weaker.

============================================================
7. CORRELATION / ASSOCIATION TEST
============================================================

Because the main predictors are categorical, Pearson correlation is not appropriate.

Primary categorical test:
- Chi-square test of independence
- Bias-corrected Cramér’s V as association strength

For the directional association test, the US outcome was grouped into:
- UP
- DOWN
- NON_DIRECTIONAL

NON_DIRECTIONAL combines:
- US_INTERNAL_LIQUIDITY_TAKE
- US_BOTH_SIDE_EXPANSION
- US_TRUE_CONTRACTION

The original five US outcomes remain unchanged in the descriptive analysis.

A. ASSOCIATION RANKING

1. Europe direction + direction-strength rank
- Cramér’s V: 0.367
- p-value: 1.65 × 10^-32
- N: 617

2. Europe direction + aligned accepted/not-accepted buffer break
- Cramér’s V: 0.365
- p-value: 9.28 × 10^-36
- N: 647

3. Europe session context: structure + direction
- Cramér’s V: 0.350
- p-value: 9.17 × 10^-31
- N: 729

4. Japan + Europe direction combination
- Cramér’s V: 0.347
- p-value: 6.43 × 10^-32
- N: 728

5. Europe direction alone
- Cramér’s V: 0.331
- p-value: 2.61 × 10^-34
- N: 729

6. Europe structure alone
- Cramér’s V: 0.287
- p-value: 9.24 × 10^-25
- N: 729

7. Japan session context
- Cramér’s V: 0.114
- p-value: 0.0069
- N: 728

8. Japan direction alone
- Cramér’s V: 0.110
- p-value: 0.00024
- N: 728

Interpretation:
- The strongest tested relationship is Europe direction combined with Europe direction strength.
- Europe direction combined with accepted buffer behavior is almost equally strong.
- Europe direction alone is the strongest simple standalone predictor.
- Europe structure is useful, but weaker than Europe direction.
- Japan variables are statistically associated with the US outcome, but their relationship is much weaker.

These relationships are moderate rather than deterministic. Statistical significance does not mean the condition is sufficient for a profitable trade.

B. DIRECTION-STRENGTH TEST

For directional Europe sessions, the ordered strength rank was tested against whether the US followed Europe.

Spearman correlation:
- Spearman rho: 0.200
- p-value: 5.15 × 10^-7
- N: 617

Continuation by Europe direction-strength rank:

LOW_DIRECTION_STRENGTH:
- Sample: 210
- US followed Europe: 44.3%

MEDIUM_DIRECTION_STRENGTH:
- Sample: 199
- US followed Europe: 62.8%

HIGH_DIRECTION_STRENGTH:
- Sample: 208
- US followed Europe: 68.3%

Medium/high versus low direction strength:
- Medium/high continuation: 65.6%
- Low continuation: 44.3%
- Odds ratio: 2.40
- Approximate 95% confidence interval: 1.71 to 3.37

Conclusion:
Europe direction strength is the clearest useful quality rank. A low-strength Europe direction is not a reliable continuation condition. Medium and high strength materially improve the probability of US continuation.

C. OTHER QUALITY RANKS

Europe range rank versus US following Europe:
- Spearman rho: 0.020
- p-value: 0.619
- No significant monotonic relationship

Europe reference-overlap rank versus US following Europe:
- Spearman rho: -0.056
- p-value: 0.163
- No significant monotonic relationship

Europe expansion-strength rank in aligned one-sided expansion sessions:
- Spearman rho: -0.041
- p-value: 0.440
- No significant monotonic relationship

Conclusion:
Range, overlap, and expansion magnitude may still describe the market state, but they do not currently provide a consistent standalone improvement in directional continuation.

============================================================
9. HYPOTHESIS DECISIONS
============================================================

H1 — Europe direction is related to US outcome:
SUPPORTED.

Evidence:
- Europe direction alone Cramér’s V = 0.331.
- p-value = 2.61 × 10^-34.
- US followed directional Europe 58.3% and directly reversed it 16.7%.

H2 — Matching Europe structure and direction strengthens the signal:
SUPPORTED.

Evidence:
- EXPANSION_UP_UP led to US up 64.3%.
- EXPANSION_DOWN_DOWN led to US down 65.9%.
- Europe combined context had Cramér’s V = 0.350, stronger than Europe direction or structure alone.

H3 — Japan agreement strengthens Europe:
SUPPORTED.

Evidence:
- Japan UP + Europe UP led to US up 65.7%.
- Japan DOWN + Europe DOWN led to US down 69.9%.
- Conflicting Japan/Europe direction reduced Europe continuation to approximately 48%–49%.

H4 — Higher Europe direction strength improves continuation:
SUPPORTED.

Evidence:
- Spearman rho = 0.200, p = 5.15 × 10^-7.
- Continuation increased from 44.3% at LOW strength to 62.8% at MEDIUM and 68.3% at HIGH.
- Medium/high strength had 2.40 times the continuation odds of low strength.

H5 — Accepted Europe breaks improve continuation:
SUPPORTED.

Evidence:
- Accepted directional breaks followed Europe 70.1%.
- Direct reversal occurred only 6.5%.
- Europe direction + accepted-break status had Cramér’s V = 0.365.

H6 — Range, expansion magnitude, and overlap are strong standalone predictors:
NOT SUPPORTED.

Evidence:
- Europe range rank: rho = 0.020, p = 0.619.
- Europe overlap rank: rho = -0.056, p = 0.163.
- Europe aligned expansion-strength rank: rho = -0.041, p = 0.440.

H7 — Open-to-close direction should outweigh conflicting structure:
PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.

Evidence:
- EXPANSION_UP_DOWN led to US down 48.8% versus US up 26.8%.
- EXPANSION_DOWN_UP was nearly balanced: US up 36.7% versus US down 33.3%.

The conflict relationship is not symmetric and should not yet be treated as a universal rule.

============================================================
10. FINAL RESEARCH CONCLUSION
============================================================

The latest research design supports a clear hierarchy:

1. Europe open-to-close direction is the primary US/NY directional bias.
2. Europe direction strength is the strongest quality rank.
3. Matching Europe structural expansion strengthens the direction.
4. Acceptance beyond the Japan 0.5% buffer is a strong confirmation.
5. Japan agreement increases conviction.
6. Japan disagreement weakens the Europe bias but does not automatically reverse it.
7. Contained Europe sessions can still provide direction, but the signal is weaker.
8. Range rank, overlap rank, and expansion-strength rank should remain descriptive context rather than core filters.
9. US true contraction is too rare to be a primary prediction target.

The strongest practical research condition is:

- Europe direction is UP or DOWN;
- Europe direction strength is MEDIUM or HIGH;
- Europe structure agrees with that direction;
- Europe accepts beyond the relevant Japan 0.5% buffer;
- Japan direction agrees with Europe.

This condition provides the clearest historical directional tendency in the dataset.

============================================================
11. IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS
============================================================

This research identifies conditional relationships, not a complete trading edge.

A US_DIRECTIONAL_UP or US_DIRECTIONAL_DOWN outcome only means that the session eventually reached the relevant structural side under the US V3 definition.

It does not establish:
- A valid entry price
- Whether adverse movement occurred first
- Whether a stop would survive
- Whether the target was reached before invalidation
- Expected R-multiple
- Transaction fees
- Slippage
- Execution latency
- Out-of-sample stability
- Cross-symbol robustness

The strongest composite conditions were selected after examining the dataset. They therefore carry data-mining risk.
