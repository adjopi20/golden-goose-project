Dynamic Sequential Observation Research — Simple Finding Report

This test observed AVAXUSDC after the first confirmed impulse bubble. The anchor was the rank-1 confirmed setup. After the anchor, the notebook observed a 900-second evidence window, entered at the end of that window, then measured future result over the next 3600 seconds. Overlapping observations were allowed for pattern discovery.

Total observations: 10,770.

Overall result:

* Median max_R_before_SL: 1.0235R
* Mean max_R_before_SL: 3.3128R
* Hit >=1R: 50.55%
* Hit >=2R: 35.44%
* Hit >=3R: 27.65%
* Hit >=5R: 18.74%
* SL touch rate: 89.50%

Main interpretation:
The dynamic rank-1 observation framework is slightly better than the previous post-rank-3 delayed-entry framework, but it still does not produce a strong standalone trading edge. The median result is only slightly above 1R, meaning many trades are still mediocre.

Useful findings:

1. Strong directional migration during the evidence window is mildly positive.
   The top 10% net_migration_R group produced better mean, median, and >=2R hit rate than baseline.

2. Market activity / volatility helps the right tail.
   High price range and high trade activity improved mean result and tail result, but did not dramatically improve the median.

3. Recovery by entry may be useful.
   Trades where price recovered from adverse movement before entry showed better mean and tail behavior.

4. Contra reaction is not automatically bad.
   High contra reaction did not clearly worsen the result. This supports the idea that contra movement can be a pullback instead of invalidation.

5. Same-side bubble count alone is not useful.
   More same-side bubbles did not meaningfully improve the median. In some combinations, too many same-side bubbles may even indicate exhaustion or a late entry.

6. Same-side price slope is not reliable in current form.
   The current slope metric has extreme outliers and should be replaced with simpler metrics such as first-to-last same-side price change, same-side new extreme count, and median same-side price position.

7. Value reacceptance using previous VAL/VAH is not useful yet.
   Most observations had no value reacceptance, and the small group with reacceptance was not clearly worse.

8. Percentile-sum metrics are broken in this run.
   bubble_percentile_score is null, causing same_side_qty_percentile_sum and opposite_qty_percentile_sum to be unusable. Tier-weight metrics still work.

Conclusion:
This branch is better than the old post-rank-3 framework, but the current metrics still do not kill enough mediocre trades. The most promising direction is not simple bubble count. The better direction is to test interaction conditions: strong directional migration, enough market activity, efficient same-side price movement, and recovery from pullback before entry.

Next action:
Fix the percentile-score issue, replace unstable same-side slope with more robust price-migration metrics, and test whether strong directional expansion within a shorter configurable sub-window can filter out mediocre trades.

Best interpretation

The 300s / 30s version tells us:

Shorter evidence horizon is not better overall.
It makes inactive/no-bubble conditions look worse.
It slightly improves tail behavior when the market is active.
But it weakens the directional-migration signal compared with 900s.

So my conclusion is:

300s is useful as an urgency/activity detector,
but not enough as the full evidence window.

The more promising design may be:

Use 300s to detect fast activity/urgency.
Use 900s to confirm whether the auction actually accepts the new price level.

In other words:

300s = impulse urgency
900s = auction acceptance
What this means for your “kill mediocre trades” idea

Yes, possible, but not by using many same-side bubbles.

The better filter shape is:

1. price moves directionally within 300s
2. evidence window has high range / high trade activity
3. price is not unresolved adverse by entry
4. recovery_by_entry_R is strong
5. do not require too many same-side bubbles

Final conclusion

This 300s / 30s run is not a breakthrough, but it gives one useful insight:

The market needs activity and directional range quickly after the first confirmed bubble. But same-side bubble count alone is not the answer.
